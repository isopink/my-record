Evacuation Simulation

Full write-up

Coupled evacuation and smoke simulation of the fourth floor of the Gangil-dong public complex building design

Course project record, Architectural engineering design course, November 2026. All modelling, simulation runs and reporting were done by the author alone; one teammate used the results. Results report dated 2026-11-14.

Abstract

The fourth floor of a public complex building design includes a senior room used as a refuge for 27 occupants who cannot use stairs. An earlier run found smoke ahead of occupants everywhere. A Pathfinder model of 136 occupants supplied the refuge door closing time (228 s) to four FDS scenarios run for 1,200 s on a 0.25 m grid. In the baseline, hall visibility at 1.8 m fell below 5 m at 85 s; the last occupant left at 257.0 s. Of 28 route checks, 27 failed. The refuge room kept 5 m visibility to 720 s only with supply pressurization or an open void top. Only one floor, one fire and one evacuation run per case were simulated.

1. Overview

This record covers a coupled evacuation and smoke simulation of the fourth floor of the Gangil-dong public complex building design, made for a course project in November 2026; the author did all of the work alone. Pathfinder computes when the last occupant passes each route point; FDS computes when visibility at the same point falls below 5 m. Both models use one floor plan, one door set and one time origin (ignition at t = 0 s). The output is a pass or fail judgement for seven route points under four smoke scenarios, and for the refuge room until fire service rescue at 720 s. Deliverables were a 12-page results report, a results workbook, seven figures and the model input files (report, page 12).

2. Problem define

The building is a design of a public complex building in Gangil-dong; this study covers its fourth floor only: 401 hall (the fire room), 402 children's library, 403 senior room, 404 administration office and 407 corridor. The floor holds 136 occupants, including children, older adults, 24 older adults who need assistance and 3 wheelchair users.

A first run on 3 November 2026, on the 31 October Revit plan, sent every occupant to the stairs. It found that smoke arrived before people at every measurement point (ASET < RSET), that the fire room took 200 s to empty, that the floor took 321 s to clear, and that every route passed through the fire room. The design then changed to a phased strategy: the 27 occupants who need assistance shelter in the 403 senior room, which serves as the refuge, while the others leave by two exits. The question for the rerun was whether the refuge room keeps tenable conditions until fire service rescue starts, set at 720 s (range 540 to 750 s), and whether the new plan, which adds a direct door from the library to the corridor, changes the route result.

The earlier method failed in two ways the report names: the evacuation and smoke models did not share the same plan, doors and time origin, and the refuge door stayed open for the whole smoke run.

In the baseline and scenario A, smoke enters the refuge room before the door closes at 228 s, and centre visibility stays between about 3 and 5 m up to 720 s. Replacing the door with a fire door alone (A) gives almost the same result as the baseline. Supply air from 60 s (B) keeps smoke out while the door is open. With the void top open, little smoke enters the room. Temperature and CO stayed far below their limits in all four scenarios.

Scenario B reached a pressure difference of about 11 Pa after the door closed, not the 40 Pa target. The report gives the cause as a wrong coefficient in the leakage calculation used to set the post-closing supply of 0.0245 m3/s; about 0.041 m3/s would be needed for 40 Pa at 0.005 m2 leakage. This does not change the visibility result, but B has not been rerun with the corrected supply.

4. Process

4.1 Sequence and the step that failed

  1. A first run on 3 November 2026 used the 31 October plan, sent all occupants to the stairs, kept the refuge door open and ran FDS for 600 s. Its evacuation and smoke models did not share one plan, door set and time origin, so the times could not be compared point by point. It was replaced by the rerun below.
  2. The fourth floor of the 13 November Revit plan was exported as IFC and modelled in Pathfinder for a 27-person and a 50-person refuge case.
  3. The Pathfinder refuge entry time (227.7 s) was used as the FDS door closing time. The baseline and a fire door alone (A) did not keep the refuge room clear enough, so the author added supply pressurization (B) with a 40 Pa target, and a void open comparison. All four scenarios were run for 1,200 s.
  4. Scenario B reached 11 Pa instead of 40 Pa because the leakage coefficient used to set the post-closing supply was wrong. The corrected supply (about 0.041 m3/s) has not been run.

4.2 Approach

Two models were built from one plan and run on one time origin (fire ignition at t = 0 s). Pathfinder gives, for each route point, the time the last occupant passes (required safe egress time, RSET). FDS gives, for the same points, the time visibility at 1.8 m falls below 5 m (available safe egress time, ASET). A point passes when ASET is later than RSET. The refuge room passes when visibility at its centre stays at or above 5 m until 750 s. The Pathfinder time at which the last refuge occupant enters (227.7 s) is used in FDS as the door closing time (228 s).

4.4 Assumptions

  • The fourth floor is modelled alone at z = 0. Evacuation ends at the two fourth floor exits; stair travel is not included.
  • Exit 1 (west) is the start of the external stair at the end of the deck, width 1.1 m. Exit 2 (core) is the core stair entrance, width 1.44 m, placed at door D-014 because the BIM model had no door between corridor and stair.
  • The refuge room is about 113 m2 and connects to the hall only by one 1.8 m double door (D-017).
  • Rooms 405 and 406 were removed because their doors were not connected in the BIM model; their occupants were placed in room 404.
  • Four administration staff were reassigned as refuge room staff, so the total stays at 136.
  • Pathfinder's assisted movement accepts only a polygon (wheelchair shape) for the assisted person, so assisted older adults are moved as wheelchair shapes.
  • The void floor opening to the floor below is closed. The void above is modelled as a closed vertical shaft up to the roof light at 9.25 m; its real opening to the fifth floor hall is not modelled.
  • Glass partitions are modelled as walls that do not break.
  • A fire door with zero gap made the FDS pressure solution unstable, so wall and frame leakage of 0.005 m2 was kept.
  • Baseline (statutory) conditions: air handling off, no smoke exhaust or pressurization on the floor, interior doors open, core fire door closed, west stair opening open to outside air.

4.8 How it is run

Pathfinder: open pf1114_base27.pth or pf1114_cmp50.pth in Pathfinder 2026 (version 2026.2.0715, steering mode); one run per case, no Monte Carlo variation. Results open from the .pfr file in Pathfinder Results.

FDS: each .fds input runs as a single MPI process. Wall clock times were 42,226 s (baseline), 42,208 s (A), 41,905 s (B) and 43,835 s (void open), each ending "FDS completed successfully" (g1114_*.out). Slices open from the .smv file in Smokeview or PyroSim Results. FDS was run only with the 27-person evacuation timeline.

5. Lesson

The evacuation side cannot be made fast enough by itself: while one group starts moving at 180 s, smoke covers the routes between 85 s and 200 s (report, page 11). The report names plan changes (a direct door from the senior room to the west exit) or hall smoke exhaust as the next steps; neither was simulated. For the refuge room, a fire door alone changed almost nothing; supply air from 60 s was what kept smoke out.

5.1 Limits

  • Only the fourth floor was modelled. Stair travel, the floors below and the fifth floor were not. In the void open scenario smoke leaves through the void toward the fifth floor; its effect there was not checked.
  • The void is a closed shaft, which gives less smoke storage than the real opening to the fifth floor hall. The report judges this conservative for the fourth floor; this was not tested.
  • One fire location, one fire size (1.5 MW) and one fuel were simulated. No sensitivity to fire growth, soot yield or grid size was run.
  • Pathfinder was run once per case with fixed inputs; there is no distribution of evacuation times. FDS used only the 27-person timeline, so the 50-person case has no smoke check.
  • Leakage areas (0.023 m2 and 0.005 m2) are assumed values, not measured. The fire door leakage differs from the team's "no gap" decision.
  • Scenario B is under-pressurized (11 Pa against 40 Pa) and needs a rerun before the 40 Pa claim can be made.
  • Glass partitions are walls that do not fail, and the senior room boundary is modelled as drywall; ordinary and fire-rated glass are not distinguished.
  • Two rooms (405, 406) were removed because of BIM door connection errors.
  • The results are simulation outputs only. No measurement, drill or validation against a real building was made, and the acceptance criteria and the 40 Pa target are not tied to a named standard in the project files; the 40 Pa value is a design target chosen by the author.
  • The report identifies that route failure follows from groups that start at 180 s; plan changes (a direct door from the senior room to the west exit) or hall smoke exhaust were proposed but not simulated.
