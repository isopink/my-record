Steps Before Crashes: elderly walking exposure and pedestrian crashes in Seoul's 467 legal dongs

Abstract

Seoul selects places for elderly pedestrian safety by past crashes or facilities, without knowing where older people walk. We join closed LG U+ walking data with closed KoROAD crash records for Seoul's 467 legal dongs, April to December 2024. Local Moran's I, stable across 5 seeds, finds 4 dongs where walking and crashes cluster together. A monthly retrained Poisson GLM forecasts next month's crashes per dong. Over July to December, its top 10 dongs held 19.0% of crashes, against 18.3% for a past-crash ranking. The model picks about as well as past crash counts; its output is a list of where to look first, with a reason for each pick.

Overview

The study asks where older people walk in Seoul and where they are hit, at the level of the 467 legal dongs. It produces walking and crash hotspots, a ranked list of 10 dongs to inspect in January 2025, and a diagnosis card per candidate. The work was entered in the 2026 Data Safe Zone Competition by a team of five. The closed data can only be opened inside the Data Safe Zone centre, so the repository holds dong-level aggregates, the analysis code and a synthetic demo.

Problem

Current selection uses past crash counts or facility requests, and residential population is not a measure of walking. The public hot-spot list has no crash type or time.

Process

Data. LG U+ flow population by sex and age (LGU_015), 50 m cells by month, walking vs driving; KoROAD traffic accidents (KRD_001), one row per victim with legal dong and hour; MOLIT (V-World) legal dong boundaries, 467 polygons; Seoul senior protection zones from data.go.kr, 195 sites, used for reference only. 10.5M cell-month rows gave 5.26M walking rows over 233,483 cells, and 1,497 crash records had a pedestrian victim aged 65 or over (April to December 2024). Source: README.md, results/numbers.json.

Method. Each cell goes to the dong containing its centre (99.42%); 636 boundary cells go by largest overlap and 720 are dropped (walkrisk/join.py). Local Moran's I on walking and on crashes, Queen contiguity, row-standardised, BH-FDR 0.05 (walkrisk/hotspots.py). Next month's crashes per dong are predicted from last month's log walking exposure, crashes and observation rate, plus the running monthly mean of crashes, with Poisson, negative binomial and LightGBM, retrained every month (walkrisk/forecast.py).

Assumptions. Missing months are kept as missing, not zero, and each dong-month carries an observation rate (mean 99.1%). Walking measured by the mobile network stands in for exposure. Protection zones are not used for selection.

Validation. Time: July to December predicted, each month trained only on earlier months. Space: one of five regions of districts held out at a time. Both against a baseline that ranks dongs by past crashes. A hotspot counts only if significant in all 5 seeds with 9,999 permutations, because 999 permutations gave between 0 and 8 dongs depending on the seed. Residual Moran's I is 0.065 (p = 0.0209), so some spatial structure remains in the errors (results/numbers.json, p20).

Lesson

The model picks about as well as past crash counts: 198 against 190 of 1,040 crash records, and the order flips with how ties are broken. The claim is therefore not that it beats history, but that it is as good as history before crashes pile up and gives a reason for each pick. The output is a list of where to look first, not a recommendation to designate a zone. Nothing here measures whether an intervention reduces crashes. The residual spatial autocorrelation and district ratios of actual to predicted crashes from 0.57 (Seongdong) to 1.65 (Dongdaemun) show where the model misses (results/numbers.json, extra).
