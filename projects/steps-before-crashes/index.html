Overview

A dong-level analysis joining closed mobile-network walking data with closed crash records for Seoul's 467 legal dongs. It outputs walking and crash hotspots and a monthly list of 10 dongs to inspect first.

The 233,483 walking cells, 5.26 million rows in all, were assigned to dongs by their centre and then by overlap, with missing months kept as missing. Poisson, negative binomial and LightGBM models predict next month's crashes from lagged walking exposure, crashes and observation rate, and are retrained every month. To validate, July to December were predicted from earlier months only, and five district regions were held out in turn, each time against a ranking by past crashes.

Data
LG U+ flow population (50 m cell by month) and KoROAD crash records, April to December 2024
Method
Local Moran's I hotspots, Poisson GLM forecast retrained monthly
Ran on
the Data Safe Zone centre; only dong-level aggregates left it

Motivation

Seoul picked places by past crash counts or by facilities, and neither tells you where older people actually walk. Residential population is not walking, and public hot-spot lists lack crash type and time.

  • 50 of Seoul's 59 elderly-pedestrian crash hot spots in 2024 were more than 300 m from the nearest senior protection zone.
  • 346 of 463 counted dongs have no senior protection zone and hold 442 of 1,455 crashes (30.4%).

Problem

The first hotspot test was unstable: Local Moran's I with 999 permutations gave anywhere from 0 to 8 dongs, depending on the random seed.

Solution

A dong now counts as a hotspot only if it is significant in all 5 seeds at 9,999 permutations. On top of the hotspots, the analysis forecasts next month's crashes per dong and gives a district office 10 dongs to inspect first, each with a diagnosis card.
