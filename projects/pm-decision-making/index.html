Overview

This is a course the author took, kept as a record of what was covered and learned. It is coursework, not a product, and no product was managed or shipped in it.

Course
AI-driven MVP Design & Product Decision Making (Korean title: AI 기반 MVP 설계와 PM 의사결정 실습).
Run by
BDAI student society, regular class, PM track.
Term
weekly on Tuesday, 20:00 to 21:00. Week 3 slides are dated 2026-09-15 in the PDF metadata, which is a Tuesday.
Length
16 weeks planned in the syllabus. The source holds material for weeks 1 to 6 only (7 recordings, 3 slide decks).
Format
live online lectures on Zoom with in-class exercises on one fictional case, a delivery app called Baedalgo with made-up internal data; recordings were provided for one week after each class.
Assessment
the syllabus states attendance rules only (camera on, more than 10 minutes late counts as absent, up to 5 absence forms).

Process

Syllabus plan, one line per week (syllabus PDF, pages 2 and 3).

  1. Week 1. Orientation; the role of a PM and of an MVP.
  2. Week 2. PM roles in big tech compared with Korean companies.
  3. Week 3. Problem definition: turning a problem into a question.
  4. Week 4. Writing a PRD; how business, marketing, engineering and design each read it.
  5. Week 5. The MVP concept: minimum features compared with the minimum set of decisions.
  6. Week 6. MVP scope: deciding what is in and what is out.
  7. Week 7. Planning an MVP with AI tools.
  8. Week 8. MVP structure: minimum screens, data and flows.
  9. Week 9. No-code tools: Softr, Tally, Airtable.
  10. Weeks 10 and 11. Building a no-code MVP, parts 1 and 2.
  11. Week 12. Reading MVP results and deriving the next decision.
  12. Week 13. Turning the MVP into a portfolio piece.
  13. Weeks 14 and 15. Feedback sessions on the final MVP.
  14. Week 16. Final presentation and review.
  1. Week 1. What a PM does, three common myths about the role, and how a feature passes from planning to design, development and QA.
  2. Week 2. A decision frame of problem, evidence, decision; four kinds of evidence (data, user voice, business context, constraints) and what makes evidence strong.
  3. Week 3. Turning app reviews into a problem statement: a codebook, a nine-column data table, counter-evidence and gaps, done in Gemini Notebook on the Baedalgo case.
  4. Week 4. What a PRD is and who reads each section; picking one root-cause hypothesis by impact and feasibility, then setting features and scope in and out.
  5. Week 5. The MVP as the smallest test of a hypothesis; RAT compared with MVP; testing the riskiest assumption without building, for example with a fake door or Wizard of Oz.
  6. Week 6. Writing PRDs so the team does not keep asking again, meeting notes, and how AI speech recognition works. No assignment that week.
  1. Week 3 to 4 exercise. Write a PRD for the Baedalgo case in a Notion template: the problem statement was filled in by the course; goals and metrics, target user, scope in and out, user stories and release plan were left for students. Submission: a Notion link and three risks.

Lesson

What the covered units trained, stated as abilities.

  1. Can frame a product issue as problem, evidence and decision, and say which of the four kinds of evidence a claim rests on. Week 2.
  2. Can turn a pile of user reviews into a problem statement with a codebook and a checkable table, keeping observation apart from hypothesis. Week 3.
  3. Can write a PRD from a template: problem statement with current-state numbers, success metrics, target user, scope in and out with a reason for each line, user stories and a release plan. Week 4.
  4. Can choose one root-cause hypothesis by impact and feasibility and defer the others, with the reason written down. Week 4.
  5. Can name the riskiest assumption behind a feature and pick a test that needs no product, such as a fake door or Wizard of Oz. Week 5.

Week 2 covered evidence instead of PM roles in big tech, and the week 6 recording covered collaboration and speech recognition instead of MVP scope.
